Post-Construction Cleaning After a Renovation: What It Involves

4 November 2026 · 4 min read · By the Tip Top team

Post-construction cleaning is the detailed clean done after building or renovation work, once the trades have finished. It removes fine construction dust from every surface, including inside cupboards, light fittings and AC grilles, and deals with residues a normal clean doesn't: paint splashes, grout haze, adhesive, silicone smears and protective films. It usually takes longer than a standard deep clean and often needs more than one pass, because dust keeps settling for some time after the work stops.

What post-construction cleaning includes

The exact scope depends on the work done, but a full post-construction clean generally covers four types of task.

1. Debris and dust removal

  • Remaining debris, offcuts and packaging collected (heavy builders' waste is usually the contractor's job)
  • Floors vacuumed with equipment suited to fine dust, before any wet cleaning
  • Dust removed from walls, ceilings, cornices and the tops of doors and frames
  • Light fittings, switches, sockets and AC grilles dusted and wiped
  • Inside every cupboard, drawer and wardrobe vacuumed and wiped

2. Residue removal

  • Paint splashes and overspray removed from glass, frames, tiles and fittings
  • Grout and cement haze cleaned from new tiles
  • Silicone smears, adhesive and sticker residue removed from sanitary ware, glass and appliances
  • Protective films peeled from windows, frames and appliances, with any glue left behind cleaned off

3. Detailed cleaning

  • Kitchen cabinets, worktops, sink and appliances cleaned inside and out
  • Bathrooms cleaned and sanitised, including tiles, glass and fittings
  • Windows, frames, sills and sliding tracks cleaned
  • Doors, skirting and handles wiped
  • Balconies and terraces swept and washed

4. Final pass

Because construction dust is so fine, it hangs in the air and settles again over hours or days. A final wipe-down of horizontal surfaces and a last floor clean, ideally after the dust has had time to settle, is what makes the difference between "clean" and "move-in ready".

In short, a deep clean assumes the surfaces are finished and just dirty. A post-construction clean assumes they're new but covered in things that don't belong there.

Why the methods matter

New finishes are easy to damage. A few common mistakes:

  • Dry-wiping heavy dust can scratch glass, polished stone and high-gloss cabinets. Dust should be vacuumed or lifted first.
  • Scraping paint off glass with the wrong blade or angle leaves permanent scratches.
  • Acidic grout-haze removers can etch marble and natural stone. They need to be matched to the surface.
  • Household vacuums can struggle with very fine construction dust, and filters clog quickly.

Book it after the last trade leaves. If the electrician returns to fit lights or the painter does touch-ups after the clean, expect dust to return. Do a snagging walk-through with your contractor first, and schedule the clean once everything is signed off.

Don't forget the AC and soft furnishings

In UAE homes, AC systems often keep running during renovation, and fine dust gets drawn into the ducts even when vents are covered. If your AC smells dusty or blows dust after the work, consider an AC duct and coil clean. If you renovated while living at home, sofas, curtains and mattresses may also have collected dust and need their own cleaning. And if you redecorated a rented flat before leaving, the post-construction clean becomes part of your move-out cleaning.

How long it takes

Time depends on the size of the property, how much work was done and how dusty it was left. A single-room refurbishment is a very different job from a full villa renovation. A company should be able to give you an estimate after seeing the property or photos.

What affects the price

Post-construction cleaning has no fixed price list, because the work varies so much. The main factors are:

  • the size and type of property
  • the scale of the building work and the amount of dust left behind
  • how much paint, grout, adhesive and film needs removing
  • the amount of glass, and access to it
  • whether extras such as AC cleaning or soft-furnishing cleaning are needed
