Home Disinfection and Sanitisation in Sharjah: When It's Worth Booking

4 November 2026 · 4 min read · By the Tip Top team

Most homes in Sharjah don't need professional disinfection every month. Regular cleaning, plus sanitising the surfaces people touch most, is enough for everyday life. Disinfection services in Sharjah are worth booking at particular moments: after someone in the home has been ill, before a new baby arrives or a vulnerable relative moves in, after moving into a new property, or after a leak, pest problem or pet accident. Knowing the difference between cleaning, sanitising and disinfecting helps you decide what you actually need.

Cleaning vs sanitising vs disinfecting

These words are often used as if they mean the same thing. In general terms, they don't.

TermWhat it doesTypical use
CleaningPhysically removes dirt, dust, grease and many germs from a surface, usually with detergent, water and wipingEvery surface, every time; it always comes first
SanitisingReduces the number of germs on a surface to a lower levelKitchen counters, chopping areas, children's play surfaces
DisinfectingUses a disinfectant product to kill a wider range of germs on hard surfaces, following the product's instructionsBathrooms, door handles, light switches, after illness

The key point: disinfecting doesn't replace cleaning. Disinfectants work poorly on dirty surfaces, because grime shields germs from the product. A proper disinfection service always cleans first.

When disinfection services in Sharjah are worth booking

  • After illness in the household, once the person has recovered, to give shared areas a thorough reset. Follow any advice from your doctor too.
  • Before a newborn comes home, or before an elderly or unwell relative moves in.
  • Moving into a new home, where you don't know how well the previous occupants cleaned.
  • After a water leak or flooding, once the area is dry, alongside any repair work.
  • After a pest problem, once the pest control treatment is complete and you've been told it's safe to clean.
  • Pet accidents or a sick pet, where hard surfaces need more than a mop.
  • Shared accommodation, offices and shops, where many people touch the same surfaces each day.

What a home disinfection service usually covers

A sensible service concentrates on the areas that matter most rather than spraying the whole home:

  1. High-touch points: door handles, light switches, remote controls, taps, toilet flush handles, fridge and cupboard handles, stair rails.
  2. Bathrooms: toilets, basins, showers, floors around the toilet and drains.
  3. Kitchen: worktops, sink, appliance handles and the bin area.
  4. Bedrooms and living areas: bedside tables, desks, phone and tablet surfaces (with suitable products), children's hard toys.

Each area is cleaned first, then sanitised or disinfected with a product suitable for that surface, left for the contact time on its label, and wiped or rinsed where the label says so.

Safety at home: never mix cleaning or disinfecting products, especially bleach with anything else. Open windows, keep children and pets out of the room while products are in use, and always follow the label.

Soft furnishings, AC and the wider home

Disinfectant sprays are mostly designed for hard surfaces. Sofas, mattresses, carpets and curtains need different treatment, usually professional cleaning suited to the fabric. Surfaces that need care

Some disinfectants can damage finishes. Natural stone is the most common problem in Sharjah villas: acidic products and some harsh chemicals etch marble and leave dull marks. Ask what will be used on marble, wood, stainless steel and screens. If your marble is already dull, our article on marble floor polishing explains how the shine is restored.

What to ask before you book

  • Will you clean the surfaces before sanitising or disinfecting them?
  • Which products will you use, and are they safe around children and pets?
  • Which rooms and touch points are included?
  • Do we need to leave the home, and for how long?
  • How will you treat marble, wood and electronics?

Be cautious of any provider that promises a specific percentage of germs killed or a lasting "protective" effect. Disinfection works on the surfaces at the time; once people use the room again, regular cleaning takes over.

Keeping things hygienic day to day

  • Wipe high-touch points regularly, more often when someone is unwell.
  • Clean kitchen worktops after preparing raw meat or fish.
  • Wash hand towels, tea towels and bathmats often.
  • Keep bathrooms ventilated to limit damp and mould.
  • Empty kitchen bins daily in hot weather to keep odours and pests down.
