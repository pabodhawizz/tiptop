How to Choose an Office Cleaning Company in Sharjah

4 November 2026 · 4 min read · By the Tip Top team

The right office cleaning company is one that turns up when agreed, cleans to a written standard you can check, and fixes problems without you having to chase. In Sharjah you'll find everything from one-person outfits to large facility firms, so the best way to choose is to compare them on the same handful of questions rather than on price alone.

This guide walks through what to look for, what to ask and the warning signs that a provider isn't a good fit for your workplace.

Start with what your office actually needs

Before you contact anyone, write down a short brief. It makes quotes comparable and shows you quickly which companies listen.

  • Size and layout: floor area, number of desks, meeting rooms, washrooms and kitchens or pantries.
  • Hours: whether cleaning must happen before opening, after closing or while staff are working.
  • Frequency: daily, a few times a week, or a one-off deep clean.
  • Special areas: glass partitions, carpets, a reception that clients see, server or storage rooms that need care.
  • Building rules: access times, loading bays, security passes and whether the landlord or building management has its own requirements for contractors.

If you aren't sure how often each area needs attention, our guide to daily, weekly and deep-clean office schedules is a useful starting point.

What to look for in an office cleaning company

Written checklists and a clear scope

A professional company works from a checklist that names each area and task: desks, high-touch points, washrooms, pantry, floors, bins, glass. Ask to see it. If the scope is only described in a sentence or two, you'll have nothing to point to when something is missed.

Trained, vetted and insured staff

Cleaners will be in your premises, often out of hours, near equipment and documents. Ask whether staff are background-checked, how they're trained and whether the company carries insurance. A good provider answers this without hesitation.

Supervision and inspection

Ask who checks the work and how often. Regular inspections, ideally against the same checklist, are what keep standards from slipping after the first few weeks.

Products and equipment

Offices are shared spaces, and strong chemical smells are a common complaint. Ask what products are used and whether low-odour options are available. Check that the company brings the right equipment for your floors and glass, rather than relying on whatever is in your cupboard.

A simple way to raise problems

Find out who your contact is, how quickly they respond and what happens if something isn't done. A named contact and a clear re-clean policy are worth more than a long list of promises.

Questions to ask before you sign

  1. Will you visit the office, or review photos and a floor plan, before quoting?
  2. What exactly is included at each visit, and what costs extra?
  3. Will the same team come each time?
  4. How are staff vetted and trained, and are they insured?
  5. Who inspects the work, and how often?
  6. What happens if a task is missed?
  7. How much notice do you need to change the schedule or add a deep clean?
  8. Can you work around our opening hours and the building's access rules?

Many of the same principles apply whether you're hiring for an office or a home; our general guide on how to choose a cleaning company in Sharjah covers them in more detail.

Warning signs

  • A fixed price without seeing the space. Office layouts vary too much for an accurate quote over the phone.
  • No written scope. If it isn't written down, it's hard to hold anyone to it.
  • Vague answers about staff. You should know who is coming into your premises.
  • Constant team changes. New faces every visit usually means tasks get missed.
  • No process for complaints. Every provider misses something eventually; what matters is how they fix it.

Run a short trial. If you're switching providers, start with a deep clean or a trial month. It shows you how the team works, how they communicate and whether the checklist is followed, before you commit to a longer arrangement.

How office cleaning is usually priced

Office cleaning is quoted on the size of the space, how often it's cleaned, the number of washrooms and kitchens, the type of floors and glass, and the hours the work has to fit into. One-off deep cleans are quoted separately from regular contracts. Because these factors vary so much, compare quotes line by line rather than on the headline figure, and make sure each one covers the same scope.

Regular cleaning vs a deep clean

Most offices need both. Regular cleaning keeps the space tidy and hygienic day to day; a periodic deep clean reaches the build-up that daily cleaning doesn't, such as carpets, upholstery, behind furniture and high-level dust. If you're not sure which you need right now, read when your office needs a deep clean.

The short version

Write a brief, ask every company the same questions and insist on a written scope, vetted staff and a clear way to fix problems.
