What Happens During a Professional AC Duct Cleaning Service

4 November 2026 · 4 min read · By the Tip Top team

A professional AC duct cleaning service follows a clear sequence: inspect the system, protect your home, loosen and extract the dust from inside the ducts, clean the grilles, unit and coil, then show you the result. Knowing what each step looks like helps you prepare, and makes it easy to tell a thorough job from a quick wipe of the vents.

This article walks through a visit from start to finish. For the bigger picture on why ducts get dirty and the signs to watch for, see our complete guide to AC duct cleaning in UAE homes.

Before the visit

The assessment

A good AC duct cleaning company wants to know what it's dealing with before it quotes. Expect to be asked how many indoor units you have, roughly how many vents or grilles, whether it's an apartment or villa, where the units are (above a false ceiling, in a cupboard, on the roof) and whether you've noticed smells or visible mould. Photos of the grilles and the unit's access panel help a lot.

Preparing your home

  • Clear space under each vent and around the indoor unit, so the team can set up a ladder.
  • Move fragile items and electronics away from the work areas.
  • Find the access panel for the unit, if you know where it is.
  • Check with your building management whether contractors need a pass or must use a service lift.
  • Plan for pets to be in a closed room away from the work and the noise.

Step by step: the AC duct cleaning service

1. Walk-through and inspection

The technician confirms the number of units and vents, looks at the filter and coil, and checks a sample of the ducting. In many systems this includes a look inside the duct, sometimes with a camera, so you can see the starting condition.

2. Switching off and protecting the room

The AC is switched off for the duration. Floors and furniture beneath the vents are covered, and the team sets up its extraction equipment.

3. Sealing and setting up extraction

Vents that aren't being worked on may be covered so loosened dust doesn't escape into other rooms. A high-powered vacuum is connected to the duct system to create suction, so debris is pulled out rather than pushed into the house.

4. Agitating the dust

Rotating brushes, flexible whips or compressed-air tools are fed through the ducts to dislodge dust, sand and lint from the inside walls. This is the part you'll hear most. The extraction keeps running, collecting what's loosened.

5. Grilles and diffusers

Each grille is taken down, washed to remove dust streaks and grime, dried and refitted.

6. Indoor unit, filter and coil

The filter is cleaned or replaced. The cooling coil is cleaned to remove the layer of dirt that blocks airflow, and the drain tray and line are cleared so condensation runs away properly. Our article on AC coil cleaning explains why this part matters so much in summer.

7. Sanitising, where needed

If there's mould or a persistent smell, an HVAC-suitable sanitiser may be applied. Ask what product is used and how long to ventilate afterwards.

8. Testing and handover

The AC is switched back on and airflow is checked at each vent. A thorough team shows you before-and-after photos and points out anything that needs separate attention, such as damaged ducting or a unit that needs mechanical repair.

Ask to see the inside of a duct before and after. Clean grilles are easy to show off; the real work is inside the ducts. Photos from inside are the clearest proof the job was done.

How long it takes

Time depends mainly on the number of units and vents, how dirty the system is and how easy the access is. A small apartment with one or two units takes far less time than a multi-storey villa with several systems. A good company gives you an estimate at assessment and sizes the team so the job is finished in one visit. You don't usually need to leave home, but you'll want to stay out of the room being worked on.

What to expect afterwards

  • Some homes notice less dust on surfaces near the vents and a fresher smell when the AC starts.
  • Airflow from previously weak vents may improve if they were blocked.
  • If a sanitiser was used, ventilate as advised.
  • Keep the filter clean between visits so dust doesn't build up again as quickly.

Red flags during the service

  • No extraction equipment: brushing without suction just moves dust around your home.
  • Only the grilles are cleaned, with nothing done inside the ducts or at the unit.
  • Heavy spraying of fragrance to make the system smell clean rather than removing the cause.
  • No before-and-after evidence, or reluctance to show you the inside of the duct.
  • A price that changes on the day without a clear reason.
