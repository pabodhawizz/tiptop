Commercial Cleaning for Shops, Clinics and Restaurants: What a Contract Should Cover

4 November 2026 · 3 min read · By the Tip Top team

A commercial office cleaning service contract should spell out exactly what gets cleaned, how often, when, by whom and how problems are put right. For shops, clinics and restaurants the details matter even more than in a standard office, because customers see the results and each type of premises has its own priorities. A clear contract protects both sides and makes quotes easy to compare.

Why the contract matters more than the quote

Two quotes can look similar and cover very different work. One might include washrooms, glass and floor care at every visit; another might treat them as extras. Before you compare office cleaning quotes, make sure each provider is pricing the same scope. The contract, or a written scope attached to it, is where that scope lives.

What every commercial office cleaning service contract should cover

  • Scope of work: a room-by-room or area-by-area list of tasks.
  • Frequency: which tasks are daily, weekly, monthly or periodic.
  • Hours: when cleaning happens, and what happens if access isn't available.
  • Staffing: how many cleaners, whether the same team attends, and how staff are vetted and trained.
  • Products and equipment: who supplies them, and any products you want avoided.
  • Consumables: whether soap, paper and bin liners are included or supplied by you.
  • Inspection and reporting: who checks the work and how issues are logged.
  • Re-cleans and complaints: what happens when something is missed, and how quickly.
  • Insurance: confirmation that the provider is insured.
  • Notice and changes: how to add extra visits, pause service or end the agreement.

Retail shops: what to add

In a shop, the cleaning has to fit around trading hours and customers notice everything at eye level.

  • Entrances and shopfront glass, which collect fingerprints and, in Sharjah, fine dust and sand.
  • Display units, shelving and mirrors, cleaned without disturbing stock.
  • Fitting rooms where relevant.
  • Floor care suited to the surface, with attention to heavy-traffic areas near the door and tills.
  • Timing before opening or after closing, so there are no wet floors while customers are in.

Clinics: what to add

Clinics and other healthcare premises usually have their own infection-control policies, and sometimes requirements set by the relevant health authority. The contract should be built around those, not the other way round.

  • Waiting areas and receptions with frequent cleaning of high-touch points: door handles, chairs, counters and card machines.
  • Washrooms cleaned and sanitised more often than in a typical office.
  • Clear boundaries on which rooms the cleaning team covers and which are handled by clinical staff.
  • Product choice agreed with the clinic, including any products it requires or prohibits.

Clinic owners: check your own infection-control policy, and with the relevant authority if you're unsure, before agreeing a scope. Clinical waste and specialist medical cleaning are separate matters and should be confirmed explicitly, not assumed.

Restaurants and cafés: what to add

Restaurants need front-of-house to look spotless and back-of-house to be properly degreased.

  • Dining areas: tables, chairs, upholstered seating, floors and under-table areas.
  • Kitchens: degreasing of surfaces, splashbacks, the exterior of equipment and hoods, plus floors and drains.
  • Washrooms for customers and staff.
  • Periodic deep cleans of the kitchen, scheduled when the restaurant is closed or quiet.
  • Upholstery, which absorbs food smells and spills over time.

Daily, periodic and one-off work

A good contract separates routine tasks from periodic ones. Daily or weekly cleaning keeps the premises presentable; periodic work, such as carpet and upholstery cleaning, high-level dusting, glass and window cleaning or a full deep clean, is scheduled less often and priced separately. If you're not sure how to split them, see our guide to daily, weekly and deep-clean schedules and our article on when a deep clean is needed.

Getting comparable office cleaning quotes

  1. Write a one-page brief listing areas, opening hours and how often you want each area cleaned.
  2. Ask each provider to visit or review photos and a floor plan before quoting.
  3. Ask for the quote to be broken down by routine and periodic work.
  4. Check what's excluded, not just what's included.
  5. Ask how they handle missed tasks and who your contact will be.
