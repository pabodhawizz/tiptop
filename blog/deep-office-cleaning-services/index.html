When Your Office Needs a Deep Clean, Not Just Daily Cleaning

4 November 2026 · 3 min read · By the Tip Top team

Daily cleaning keeps an office tidy, but it isn't designed to reach the dust, grime and build-up that collect in places nobody touches every day. Deep office cleaning services go further: carpets, upholstery, high-level surfaces, behind and under furniture, kitchens and washrooms cleaned in detail. Most offices benefit from one periodically, and some situations call for one straight away.

Daily cleaning vs a deep clean

The difference is depth, not effort. A daily or regular clean focuses on what gets used and seen: emptying bins, wiping desks and high-touch points, cleaning washrooms and the pantry, and vacuuming or mopping open floor. A deep clean covers the areas that routine cleaning skips.

Signs your office needs a deep clean

  • Dust returns quickly on desks and shelves, even after the daily clean.
  • Carpets look dull or stained, especially along walkways and under desks.
  • Chairs and sofas look grubby or hold food and coffee smells.
  • The pantry smells, or the fridge and microwave haven't been cleaned inside for months.
  • Washrooms look tired, with limescale on taps and marks in the grout.
  • Visible dust on AC grilles and light fittings, which is common in Sharjah where fine dust and sand find their way into every building.
  • Staff are complaining about dust, smells or general grubbiness.

When to book one

Moving into or out of an office

An empty office is the easiest time to deep clean. Moving in, you start with a clean slate; moving out, a thorough clean helps with the handover to the landlord.

After fit-out or renovation

Construction dust settles on every surface and gets into soft furnishings and AC grilles. A deep clean, or a dedicated post-construction clean, should come after the trades have finished and before staff return.

Before an important visit or event

Client visits, audits, open days and launches are good reasons to schedule a deep clean a day or two beforehand.

As part of a regular schedule

Many offices book a deep clean every few months, on top of routine cleaning, so build-up never gets out of hand. How often depends on headcount, footfall and how dusty the location is. Our guide to office cleaning schedules explains how to fit periodic deep cleans around daily and weekly work.

After illness goes round the office

If several people have been off sick, a deep clean with attention to high-touch points and shared areas can be a sensible reset. It doesn't replace staying home when unwell, but it helps.

What deep office cleaning services usually include

  1. High-level dusting: tops of cabinets, partitions, light fittings and AC grilles.
  2. Workstations cleaned around and behind equipment, with care around electronics.
  3. High-touch points sanitised: handles, switches, lift buttons, phones and shared devices.
  4. Kitchen or pantry degreased, inside appliances and cupboards.
  5. Washrooms descaled and sanitised in detail.
  6. Glass partitions, doors and interior windows cleaned.
  7. Carpets and upholstered chairs or sofas cleaned.
  8. Floors cleaned edge to edge, including under furniture that can be moved.

Plan around your IT. Ask staff to clear desks and lock away documents before the deep clean, and tell the cleaning team about any equipment that mustn't be moved or unplugged.

Keeping disruption low

Deep cleans are usually scheduled when the office is empty, such as on a closing day or out of hours. Agree access times with your building management in advance, especially if the team needs a service lift or loading bay. A well-sized team can complete most offices in one visit, so staff come back to a finished job rather than a half-done one.
