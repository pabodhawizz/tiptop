Kitchen Deep Cleaning: What Professionals Do That Weekly Cleaning Misses

4 November 2026 · 4 min read · By the Tip Top team

Kitchen deep cleaning goes after the grease, limescale and grime that weekly wiping leaves behind: the hood and its filters, the tops of wall cabinets, cupboard interiors, the backsplash grout, the sink drain and the gaps beside appliances. Weekly cleaning keeps the surfaces you use every day looking clean; a deep clean deals with what builds up out of sight. Here's what professionals do differently, and how to tell when your kitchen needs it.

Why kitchens need more than weekly cleaning

Cooking releases fine droplets of oil that settle on every surface, not just the hob. In a warm kitchen that oil catches dust and turns sticky, then hardens into a film that ordinary spray cleaners barely touch. In UAE homes, hard water adds limescale to taps and sinks, and fine sand finds its way onto the tops of cabinets and into window tracks. None of this is a sign of a dirty household; it simply builds up faster than weekly cleaning can remove it.

The hood and filters

The extractor hood is usually the greasiest thing in the kitchen. Metal mesh filters can be soaked in a hot degreasing solution and scrubbed; the inside of the canopy is degreased by hand. A clean filter also helps the hood actually pull steam and smells out of the room.

Cabinet tops and fronts

The tops of wall cabinets, out of sight above eye level, collect a sticky layer of grease and dust. Cabinet fronts and handles gather fingerprints and cooking residue. Professionals use a degreaser suited to the surface, so lacquered or wooden finishes aren't damaged.

The sink and drain

Limescale builds up around the tap base and drainer, and food residue collects in the drain and overflow, causing smells. A deep clean descales the sink and taps and cleans the drain fittings.

How professionals approach it

  1. Clear and protect. Worktops are cleared and anything that shouldn't get wet is moved.
  2. Top down. Light fittings, cabinet tops and the hood first, so grease and dust fall onto areas not yet cleaned.
  3. Let products work. Degreaser and descaler are given time to soften residue, rather than relying on hard scrubbing that scratches surfaces.
  4. The right tool for the surface. Non-scratch pads for stainless steel and glass hobs, softer cloths for lacquer and natural stone.
  5. Rinse and dry. Residue is wiped away so surfaces don't feel tacky afterwards.
  6. Floors last, edge to edge.

Marble or granite worktops? Acidic cleaners, including vinegar and many limescale removers, can etch natural stone. Tell the team what your worktops are made of, and use a pH-neutral cleaner on them day to day.

Signs your kitchen is due a deep clean

  • Cabinet tops or the hood feel sticky to the touch
  • The hood isn't clearing smoke or smells as well as it used to
  • White crust around the taps or in the sink
  • A smell from the drain that comes back after cleaning
  • Discoloured grout behind the hob
  • You're moving in or out, or the kitchen hasn't had a thorough clean in months

Keeping it clean afterwards

  • Wipe the hob and splashback after cooking, while grease is still soft.
  • Run the hood while you cook and for a few minutes after.
  • Wash the hood filters regularly, before grease hardens.
  • Dry the sink and taps after use to slow limescale.
  • Wipe cabinet handles weekly; they collect more grime than the doors.

Food residue in hidden corners also attracts pests, so a clean kitchen is one of the best ways to keep cockroaches away.
