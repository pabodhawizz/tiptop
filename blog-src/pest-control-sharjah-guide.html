Most Sharjah homes will need pest control at some point. Cockroaches find their way up drains in apartment buildings, ants appear in kitchens in the warmer months, and bed bugs travel home in luggage. If you have young children or pets, the treatment itself is as much a worry as the pests. This guide covers what to expect and how to keep everyone safe.

Common pests in Sharjah homes

  • Cockroaches: especially in kitchens and bathrooms, and in buildings where they move between flats through shared pipes and ducts.
  • Ants: drawn to food crumbs, pet food and moisture.
  • Bed bugs: often brought in from travel or second-hand furniture; hard to clear without professional treatment. Learn the early signs of bed bugs.
  • Mosquitoes: around gardens and standing water, especially after rain.
  • Rodents: more common in villas with gardens and storage areas.

Signs you need professional help

  • You see cockroaches during the day (usually a sign of a larger population)
  • Small dark spots or shed skins along mattress seams or furniture joints
  • Droppings in cupboards or along walls
  • Ant trails that come back after cleaning
  • Bites you can't explain, especially in a line or cluster

Store-bought sprays can knock back a few visible insects, but they rarely reach where the pests breed, and some can scatter them deeper into walls or neighbouring rooms.

How professional pest control works

  1. Inspection to identify the pest, where it's coming from and how far it has spread.
  2. A treatment plan matched to the pest. That might be gel baits for cockroaches, targeted spraying, or a series of visits for bed bugs.
  3. Treatment of the affected areas and likely entry points.
  4. Aftercare advice: when it's safe to go back into rooms, what to clean and what to leave.
  5. A follow-up visit where the pest needs it. Bed bugs and heavy cockroach problems usually do.

Keeping children and pets safe

Ask these questions before any treatment:

  • Which products will be used, and are they suitable for homes with children and pets?
  • How long must people and animals stay out of treated rooms?
  • Do fish tanks, bird cages or pet bowls need to be covered or moved?
  • When can I clean treated areas, and with what?

As a general rule:

  • Keep children and pets out of treated rooms until the technician says it's safe and surfaces have dried.
  • Remove or cover pet food and water bowls, toys and children's toys before treatment.
  • Cover aquariums and switch off their air pumps if advised. Fish are very sensitive to sprays.
  • Open windows to ventilate once you're allowed back in.

How to prepare your home

  1. Clear kitchen counters and put food in sealed containers or the fridge.
  2. Empty the cupboards that will be treated, especially under the sink.
  3. Move furniture slightly away from walls if the technician asks.
  4. For bed bugs, follow the specific instructions. Bedding and clothes usually need hot washing.

After treatment: cleaning without undoing the work

It's natural to want to clean everything straight away, but wiping treated areas too soon can remove the product before it has worked. Clean food-preparation surfaces before using them, but ask before mopping skirting or wiping inside treated cupboards. Once the treatment period is over, a deep clean helps remove dead insects, droppings and the food residue that attracted pests in the first place.

Prevention: keeping pests from coming back

  • Don't leave pet food out overnight.
  • Fix dripping taps and wipe up standing water.
  • Seal gaps around pipes under sinks.
  • Empty bins daily and keep them closed.
  • Deep clean the kitchen regularly. Grease and crumbs behind appliances feed pests.

Private company or municipality?
