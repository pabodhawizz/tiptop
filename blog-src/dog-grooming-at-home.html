Grooming keeps a dog comfortable, clean and healthy. It's also a regular check for problems hiding under the coat. More and more owners in Sharjah choose to have their dog groomed at home: no car journey in the heat, no waiting room, and the dog stays somewhere it knows. Here's what a full groom involves and how to get the most out of it.

What a full groom includes

The exact steps depend on breed and coat, but a full groom generally covers:

  1. A quick health check. The groomer looks over the skin, coat, ears, eyes and paws before starting, and tells you about anything unusual.
  2. Brushing and de-matting. Loose undercoat and tangles are worked out before washing. Water tightens mats and makes them harder to remove.
  3. Bath. A dog-appropriate shampoo, washed through to the skin and rinsed thoroughly. Leftover shampoo is a common cause of itching.
  4. Drying. Dried and brushed through, so the coat isn't left damp against the skin.
  5. Nail trim. Nails trimmed to a comfortable length, avoiding the quick.
  6. Ear care. Ears checked and gently cleaned.
  7. Final brush and tidy, plus a trim where the breed and coat need one.

Why groom at home?

  • Less stress. Many dogs are calmer in their own space, without other animals, noise or a car journey.
  • No heat exposure. In the UAE summer, avoiding the trip is good for your dog and for you.
  • Easier for older or anxious dogs, and for owners without a car.
  • One visit, two jobs. You can book grooming alongside a home clean, so the house and the dog are done together.

How often should you groom your dog in the UAE?

It depends on the coat:

Coat typeTypical needs
Short, smooth coatsLess frequent full grooms; regular brushing to manage shedding
Double coats (e.g. retrievers, huskies)Regular de-shedding, especially when the coat blows. Don't shave unless a vet advises it; the coat helps regulate temperature
Long or continuously growing coats (e.g. poodles, shih tzus)Regular grooms and trims to prevent matting

For more on timing, see how often to groom your dog in the UAE heat. Whatever the coat, nails and ears need regular attention. Your groomer can suggest a schedule after the first visit.

Grooming and the heat

A clean, de-matted coat lets air move through to the skin, which helps your dog stay comfortable. Mats trap heat and moisture and can hide skin problems. If your dog has a double coat, ask your vet before shaving it. Removing the dead undercoat is usually the better approach.

How to prepare for a home groom

  • Take your dog for a short walk beforehand, at a cool time of day, so it's settled (see dog walking in the UAE heat).
  • Choose a space with good light and access to water, such as a bathroom or a utility area.
  • Tell the groomer about any health issues, sensitivities or past bad experiences.
  • Keep other pets in a separate room while the groom is going on.

Questions to ask a groomer

  1. What does the groom include for my dog's breed and coat?
  2. What products do you use?
  3. How do you handle a nervous or reactive dog?
  4. Will you tell me about anything you notice on the skin, ears or paws?
