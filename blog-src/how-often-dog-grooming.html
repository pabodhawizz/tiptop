How often your dog needs grooming depends mostly on its coat. Dogs with continuously growing coats usually need a professional groom every few weeks, double-coated dogs need regular de-shedding, and short-coated dogs need fewer full grooms but still need nails and ears done. In the UAE heat and humidity, keeping the coat brushed and mat-free matters more than following a fixed calendar.

Why the UAE climate changes the schedule

  • Heat. A brushed, mat-free coat lets air reach the skin. Mats trap heat and moisture against the body.
  • Humidity. Damp coats that don't dry properly can lead to smells and skin irritation, particularly in folds and under thick fur.
  • Air conditioning. Dogs living mostly indoors can shed on and off through the year rather than in clear seasons.
  • Sand and dust. Fine sand works into coats and between paw pads, so many dogs need more frequent brushing and paw checks.

Shaving is not the answer for a double-coated dog in summer. The coat helps regulate temperature and protects the skin from the sun. Don't shave unless a vet advises it; removing the dead undercoat is usually the better approach.

Nails, ears and teeth: on their own schedule

Nails

Nails generally need trimming every few weeks, though dogs that walk a lot on pavement may wear them down. If you can hear nails clicking on hard floors, they're probably too long.

Ears

Check ears weekly, especially floppy-eared breeds and dogs that swim. Clean gently when needed. A strong smell, redness, discharge or head shaking means a vet visit, not just a clean.

Teeth

Dental care is part of grooming in the broad sense. Ask your vet how best to look after your dog's teeth.

How often should you bathe your dog?

Most dogs don't need frequent baths. Bathing too often can strip natural oils and dry out the skin. Bath when your dog is dirty or smelly, after swimming in the sea or a pool, or as part of a professional groom. Always rinse thoroughly, because leftover shampoo can cause itching, and dry the coat fully, right down to the skin.

Signs your dog is due a groom

  • Tangles or mats, especially behind the ears, in the armpits and on the back legs
  • A smell that comes back soon after a bath
  • Hair growing over the eyes or between the paw pads
  • Nails clicking on the floor
  • Heavy shedding around the home
  • Scratching, licking or chewing at the coat or paws

If scratching or licking continues after a groom, or you notice red, flaky or broken skin, see your vet.

Regular beats rescue. A dog groomed on a steady schedule is quicker and more comfortable to groom each time. Letting a coat mat badly means a longer, harder session for your dog.

How often to book a dog groomer

For many owners, the simplest plan is to set the next appointment at the end of each groom, based on what the groomer saw that day. Life stage matters too:

  • Puppies: short, gentle sessions early on help them get used to handling, even before the coat needs much work.
  • Adult dogs: a steady routine suited to the coat.
  • Senior dogs: often benefit from more frequent, shorter grooms, as they may find long sessions tiring and may groom themselves less.
