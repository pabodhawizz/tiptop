Post-renovation cleaning is worth it in almost every case, because a builders' clean is designed to clear the site, not to make it liveable. Contractors usually remove waste and give the floors a sweep, but leave behind fine dust in cupboards and on every ledge, paint specks on glass, grout haze on tiles and dust in the AC. A proper post-renovation clean deals with all of that, so you're not wiping the same layer of dust off your furniture for weeks.

What a builders' clean usually covers, and what it misses

Every contractor is different, so check your agreement. But a typical "builders' clean" or "sweep-out" covers the basics only.

Usually done by buildersUsually left for a proper clean
Removing rubble and offcutsFine dust inside cupboards, drawers and wardrobes
Sweeping floorsDust on walls, door tops, frames, skirting and light fittings
A rough wipe of obvious messPaint specks and overspray on glass, tiles and fittings
Taking away tools, materials and packagingGrout and cement haze on new tiles
Clearing walkways and access routesSilicone, adhesive and protective-film residue
Disposing of builders' wasteDust in AC grilles, window tracks and sockets

The difference is the level of detail. Construction dust is so fine that a broom just moves it around, and it settles again on every horizontal surface within hours.

Is post-renovation cleaning worth it?

For a small job, such as repainting one room, a careful DIY clean may be enough. For anything bigger, professional cleaning usually pays for itself in time and in protecting new finishes. It's worth it when:

  • the work involved cutting tiles, sanding, drilling into concrete or removing old fittings
  • you've renovated a kitchen or bathroom, where residues are concentrated
  • you lived at home during the work and dust spread to other rooms
  • you have young children, pets or anyone with breathing sensitivities in the household
  • you're about to rent out, sell or hand back the property

Post-renovation cleaning tips for homeowners

During the renovation

  • Seal off the work area with plastic sheeting and tape over doorways, if you're living at home.
  • Cover or remove soft furnishings: sofas, rugs, curtains and mattresses hold dust that's hard to get out.
  • Protect the AC. Ask the contractor to cover vents in the work area, and change or clean filters afterwards.
  • Agree on clean-up in your contract: what the builders will remove, and what state they'll leave the site in.

After the renovation

  • Wait until all trades have finished, including snagging fixes, before booking the main clean.
  • Vacuum before you wipe. Dry dust should be lifted, not smeared or rubbed into surfaces.
  • Work from top to bottom: ceilings, light fittings and high shelves first, floors last.
  • Don't scrape glass or polished stone with the wrong tools, and test products on a small hidden area first.
  • Avoid acidic products on marble when removing grout haze; they cause dull etch marks.
  • Plan a second light clean a few days later to catch dust that settles after the first one.

Watch your health. Construction dust can irritate eyes, skin and airways. Ventilate well, wear a suitable dust mask if you're cleaning yourself, and keep children and pets away from the area. If anyone develops breathing problems, see a doctor.

Renovated a rented home?

Tenants sometimes redecorate or fix up a flat before handing it back, for example repainting walls or replacing a damaged fitting. What a professional post-renovation clean involves

  1. Fine dust vacuumed from all surfaces, including inside cupboards and on high ledges
  2. Paint, grout haze, silicone and adhesive removed with methods suited to each surface
  3. Kitchen and bathrooms cleaned in detail and sanitised
  4. Glass, frames, sills and sliding tracks cleaned
  5. Switches, sockets, AC grilles, doors and skirting wiped
  6. Floors cleaned edge to edge, with a final pass once dust has settled

What affects the price

Post-renovation cleaning has no fixed price, because no two renovations leave the same mess. The cost depends on the size of the property, how much of it was renovated, how much dust and residue was left, and whether extras such as AC duct or soft-furnishing cleaning are needed.
