Professional marble polishing services restore the shine to dull, scratched or etched marble floors, and in a busy Sharjah home that shine can last a long time if the floor is cared for properly afterwards. A typical job involves an inspection, cleaning, one or more stages of abrasive work to smooth the surface, and a final polish. How long the result lasts depends mostly on foot traffic, sand and grit, and what you clean the floor with day to day.

Signs your marble needs polishing

Marble is a soft natural stone, so it shows wear more than porcelain tiles. Look out for:

  • Dull traffic lanes in hallways, entrances and around the sofa
  • Fine scratches, often from sand carried in on shoes
  • Etch marks: dull, slightly rough spots where something acidic, such as juice, vinegar or lemon, touched the stone
  • Uneven tiles or lippage, where edges sit higher than their neighbours
  • Stains and dark grout lines that normal mopping doesn't lift

Light dullness can often be handled with a polish alone. Deeper scratches and uneven tiles usually need more work first.

What to expect from marble polishing services

Methods vary by provider and by the condition of the floor, but a professional job generally follows this order:

  1. Assessment. The floor is checked for scratches, etching, cracks, lippage and stains, and the right level of work is agreed. Not every floor needs every stage.
  2. Preparation. Furniture is moved, skirting and walls are protected, and the floor is cleaned so grit isn't ground into the stone.
  3. Grinding (if needed). Coarse diamond abrasives flatten uneven tiles and remove deep scratches. This is the most intensive stage and isn't required for every floor.
  4. Honing. Progressively finer abrasives smooth the surface and remove lighter scratches and etching.
  5. Polishing. Very fine abrasives or polishing compounds bring up the shine.
  6. Crystallising or sealing (optional). Some providers apply a crystallising treatment for extra gloss, or a penetrating sealer to help resist stains.
  7. Final clean and handover. Residue is removed and you're given care advice.

How to prepare your home

  • Clear small items, rugs and fragile pieces from the rooms being polished.
  • Let the team know about any cracked tiles, loose skirting or previous repairs.
  • Plan for the rooms to be out of use while the work is done and the floor settles.
  • Keep children and pets in another part of the home during the work.

Wet grinding and honing can be noisy, so if you live in an apartment, check with your building management about permitted working hours.

How long does the shine last?

There's no fixed answer, because it depends on how the floor is used. In general:

FactorEffect on the finish
Heavy foot traffic (entrances, kitchens, hallways)Dulls faster and may need attention sooner
Sand and grit tracked in from outsideActs like sandpaper underfoot and scratches the surface
Acidic spills and cleanersCause etch marks that break up the shine
Doormats and regular dust moppingHelp the finish last much longer
PetsClaws and accidents can add scratches and marks

Many homes find that light areas such as bedrooms keep their finish far longer than the entrance and kitchen, so it's common to re-polish busy areas more often than the rest. Our guide on how often to polish marble floors goes into the timing in more detail.

Daily care to protect the finish

Never use vinegar, lemon or other acidic cleaners on marble. Acids react with the stone and leave dull etch marks, even on a freshly polished floor. Use a pH-neutral cleaner made for stone.

  • Dust mop or vacuum daily in busy areas to remove sand before it scratches.
  • Mop with a pH-neutral cleaner and clean water, and don't leave the floor soaking wet.
  • Wipe up spills straight away, especially juice, coffee, wine, soft drinks and tomato-based food.
  • Use doormats inside and outside every entrance, including balcony doors.
  • Put felt pads under chair and table legs.
  • Avoid harsh or abrasive products, bleach-based cleaners and scouring pads.

What affects the cost

Marble polishing is quoted for each floor, because the work needed varies so much. The price depends on:

  • the area in square metres and the number of rooms or stairs
  • the floor's condition, and whether grinding is needed or just honing and polishing
  • stains, cracks or chips that need extra attention
  • whether sealing or crystallising is included
  • access, furniture moving and timing
