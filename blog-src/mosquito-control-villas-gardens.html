Effective mosquito control around a villa starts with water. Mosquitoes lay their eggs in standing water, and many of the ones that bite around homes breed in small amounts of it close by: plant saucers, buckets, blocked drains, AC condensate trays and pool covers. Removing that water every week, keeping mosquitoes out of the house and getting professional help when numbers stay high are the three parts of a plan that works.

Why villas and gardens attract mosquitoes

A villa garden in Sharjah is a pocket of water and shade in a dry landscape. Irrigation keeps soil and pots damp, dense planting gives adult mosquitoes somewhere cool to rest during the day, and outdoor features like fountains, ponds and pools add more water. After rain, puddles, gutters and anything left outside can collect water for days. Mosquitoes don't need much: a bottle cap of water can be enough for some species to breed.

Find and remove breeding sites

Walk around the villa once a week and check for standing water. Common places:

  • Plant pots and saucers: empty saucers or fill them with sand.
  • Buckets, watering cans, toys and tools left outside: turn them upside down or store them under cover.
  • AC condensate: check where AC drain lines discharge and that water drains away rather than pooling.
  • Gutters and roof drains: clear sand, leaves and debris so water doesn't sit.
  • Floor drains and gully traps on terraces and in service areas: keep covers in place and intact.
  • Pool covers: water collects on top; sweep or pump it off.
  • Ornamental ponds and fountains: keep water moving, or ask a specialist about suitable options.
  • Pet water bowls outside: empty and refill daily.
  • Water tanks: make sure lids and covers are tight.
  • Irrigation leaks and low spots in lawns where water pools.
  • Construction or storage areas: tarps, tyres and containers that hold water.

Scrub, don't just empty. Some mosquitoes lay eggs just above the waterline, on the inside of containers. When you empty a container, scrub the sides before you refill or store it.

Reduce resting places

Adult mosquitoes rest in cool, shaded, sheltered spots during the day. Trim dense shrubs and hedges, especially near doors and seating areas, clear leaf litter, and avoid overwatering. You don't need to strip the garden, just open it up a little so it dries and gets more airflow.

Keep mosquitoes out of the house

  • Fit and repair screens on windows and doors you open, and keep them closed at dawn and dusk when many mosquitoes are most active.
  • Check door seals on doors to the garden and terraces.
  • Use fans on terraces and in outdoor seating areas; mosquitoes are weak fliers.
  • Wear loose, long sleeves in the garden at dawn and dusk.
  • Use repellent if needed, following the label instructions, especially for children. Ask a pharmacist or doctor which is suitable for babies, young children or during pregnancy.

If you develop a fever or feel unwell after mosquito bites, see a doctor and mention the bites.

Pets and mosquitoes

Mosquitoes bite animals as well as people. Keep outdoor water bowls fresh, bring pets inside at dawn and dusk if mosquitoes are a problem, and ask your vet about protection that's suitable for your animal. Don't use human repellents on pets unless your vet says a product is safe for them.

When to get professional help

If you've dealt with standing water and still have a lot of mosquitoes, a professional can inspect the property to find breeding sites you've missed and advise on treatment of the garden and outdoor areas. Methods vary between providers, so ask:

  • What will be treated: breeding sites, resting areas, or both?
  • What products will be used, and are they suitable around children, pets, fish ponds and vegetable beds?
  • How long should people and animals stay out of the garden afterwards?
  • How many visits are likely, and how long the effect lasts?

A weekly mosquito routine

  1. Walk the garden, terraces and roof, and empty or cover anything holding water.
  2. Scrub the inside of containers you've emptied.
  3. Check AC drain points, gutters and floor drains.
  4. Refresh outdoor pet bowls and bird baths.
  5. After rain, do the walk again as soon as you can.
