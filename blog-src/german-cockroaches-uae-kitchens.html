German cockroaches keep coming back to UAE kitchens because they breed fast, hide deep inside appliances and cabinets, carry their eggs in a protected case, and often arrive again from neighbouring flats or in boxes and bags. A German cockroach problem usually returns when treatment only reaches the insects you can see, when there's no follow-up, or when food, moisture and gaps aren't dealt with. Understanding how they live is the key to getting rid of them for good.

How to recognise a German cockroach

  • Small: noticeably smaller than the large, dark cockroaches that come up from drains.
  • Light brown or tan, with two dark stripes running lengthways behind the head.
  • Fast-moving, and quick to vanish into gaps when a light goes on.
  • Found mainly indoors, in kitchens and bathrooms, rather than outside or in drains.
  • Young ones are even smaller and darker, without wings.

Other signs include dark, pepper-like droppings in cabinet corners and hinges, small brown egg cases, and a musty smell where there are a lot of them.

Why they love UAE kitchens

German cockroaches need warmth, moisture, food and tight places to hide, and a typical kitchen offers all four. The motor area of the fridge stays warm. The space under the sink is often damp. Crumbs collect under the toaster, behind the cooker and in the cutlery drawer. Cabinet hinges, the gaps behind the splashback and the voids under kick boards give them dark, narrow places to shelter. With the AC running and the kitchen used every day, conditions suit them year-round.

Why they keep coming back

They multiply quickly

German cockroaches breed faster than most other cockroaches. A few insects that survive a treatment can rebuild a population in a matter of weeks.

Their eggs are protected

The female carries her egg case with her until the eggs are almost ready to hatch. The case shields the eggs from many treatments, so new cockroaches can appear after a single visit. That's why follow-up treatments matter.

They hide where sprays don't reach

Most of the population is out of sight: inside appliances, behind panels, in hinges and in wall voids. Spraying open surfaces kills the few that are out, and can push the rest deeper into hiding.

They can learn to avoid some treatments

Repeated use of the same shop-bought product can leave behind the cockroaches that tolerate it best, and some populations learn to avoid certain baits. This is one reason a professional who varies the approach can succeed where repeated DIY spraying hasn't.

They're re-introduced

In apartment buildings, they move between flats along pipes and service ducts. They also arrive in cardboard boxes, grocery bags, second-hand appliances and furniture.

They still have food

Bait has to compete with everything else in the kitchen. If there's grease behind the cooker and pet food left out overnight, bait is less attractive.

What helps break the cycle

  1. Professional treatment of hiding places. Baits and targeted treatment placed in hinges, gaps, behind appliances and around pipes reach the population where it lives. For an overview of what tends to work in apartments, see cockroach control in Sharjah apartments.
  2. Follow-up visits to catch cockroaches that hatch after the first treatment.
  3. Removing food and water. Sealed containers, a clean hob every evening, no dishes left overnight, no pet food left out, and a dry under-sink area.
  4. Leaving baits alone. Don't spray over baits or wipe them away; ask your provider when it's fine to clean.
  5. Blocking entry points. Ask building maintenance to seal gaps around pipes, and keep drain covers in place.
  6. Getting rid of cardboard. Unpack deliveries and groceries promptly and take boxes out of the home.
  7. Raising it with the building, so shared areas and chutes are treated too.

Don't keep reaching for the spray can. Repeated spraying with household products rarely solves a German cockroach problem and can make it harder to treat. Never mix products. If you have children or pets, ask a professional what will be used and how to keep them safe.

A deep clean makes treatment work better

Once your provider confirms it's safe, a thorough kitchen deep clean removes droppings, egg cases, shed skins and the grease and crumbs that feed cockroaches, especially behind and under appliances, inside cabinets and around the hood. Doing it at the right time, after the treatment has had a chance to work, helps keep the kitchen clear. Tell the cleaning team where baits were placed so they can work around them.
