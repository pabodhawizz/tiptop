Good office cleaning runs on a schedule: some tasks every day, some every week and some only every few months. Splitting the work this way keeps the office hygienic and presentable without paying for more than you need. Here's a practical schedule for a typical Sharjah office, and a clear view of who is responsible for what.

Why a written office cleaning schedule helps

Without a schedule, cleaning drifts towards whatever is most visible, and the rest gets forgotten. A written plan sets expectations for the cleaning team, makes it easy to check the work and stops arguments about whether something was "included". It also makes quotes from different providers comparable.

Daily tasks

These keep the office usable and hygienic. In a busy office they're done every working day; in a small, quiet one, three times a week may be enough.

  • Empty bins and replace liners
  • Wipe desks and clear work surfaces (without moving papers)
  • Clean high-touch points: door handles, light switches, lift buttons, shared phones and printers
  • Clean and restock washrooms
  • Clean the pantry or kitchen: counters, sink, tables and appliance fronts
  • Vacuum or mop main walkways and entrances
  • Spot-clean marks on glass doors and the reception

Weekly tasks

  • Vacuum or mop all floors fully, including under desks
  • Dust shelves, window sills, skirting and furniture
  • Clean inside the microwave and wipe the fridge exterior and handles
  • Clean glass partitions and internal doors fully
  • Wipe chairs and meeting-room tables
  • Descale washroom taps and fittings

Monthly tasks

  • Clean out the office fridge
  • Dust high-level surfaces: tops of cabinets, partitions and door frames
  • Wipe AC grilles, which collect dust quickly in Sharjah
  • Clean light switches, sockets and wall marks
  • Check and treat any carpet stains

Quarterly or periodic deep cleans

Every few months, or after a specific trigger, a deep clean reaches what routine cleaning can't.

  • Carpet and upholstery cleaning
  • Kitchen degreasing inside cupboards and appliances
  • Washroom deep clean, including grout
  • Interior windows, frames and blinds
  • Under and behind furniture
  • Exterior glass and windows, where the building allows

How often depends on headcount, visitors and how dusty the location is. Our article on when your office needs a deep clean explains the signs to watch for.

Office cleaning responsibilities: who does what

Many disputes come from unclear responsibilities. Agree these in writing.

The cleaning provider

  • Carrying out the agreed tasks at the agreed frequency
  • Supplying trained staff, and usually the products and equipment
  • Inspecting work and fixing anything missed
  • Reporting damage, leaks or maintenance issues they notice

The office

  • Giving access at the agreed times
  • Asking staff to clear desks and lock away confidential papers
  • Naming one contact person for feedback
  • Agreeing who supplies consumables such as soap, paper and bin liners

Staff

  • Keeping desks clear enough to be cleaned
  • Washing their own cups, or using the agreed system
  • Labelling and clearing fridge items before the clean-out day

The building management

In many Sharjah towers, common areas such as lobbies, lifts and corridors are cleaned by the building, not the tenant. Check where your responsibility ends, and whether the building has rules for contractors, such as access hours or service-lift use.

Keep the checklist where the team can see it. A printed checklist in the cleaning cupboard, signed off at each visit, is a simple way to spot gaps early.

Adjusting the schedule over time

Review the schedule after the first month and then every few months. Add frequency where problems keep appearing, and reduce it where an area stays clean. Busy seasons, new staff or a change of layout are all reasons to revisit it.
